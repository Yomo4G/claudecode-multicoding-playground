# Assumptions — Handwritten Ephemeral SNS

This document describes the explicit and implicit assumptions
on which this service is built.
If these assumptions do not hold, the service may fail —
and that failure is considered acceptable.

## Assumptions about Humans

- Humans do not always want to be understood.
- Humans do not always want to be seen, reacted to, or evaluated.
- Humans are capable of finding meaning in ambiguity, noise, and incompleteness.
- Humans can feel relief simply by externalizing thoughts,
  even without feedback.
- Silence, absence, and inactivity are meaningful human states,
  not errors or drop-offs.
- Personality is not fully expressible through structured data,
  profiles, or metrics.

## Assumptions about Language

- Language is not only a vehicle for information.
- Handwritten text carries human presence beyond semantic meaning.
- Legibility is not a prerequisite for value.
- Words do not need to persist to be meaningful.
- Some words should exist only temporarily.
- Language becomes distorted when optimized for speed, reach, or permanence.

## Assumptions about Writing

- Writing is slower than typing, and that slowness is valuable.
- Writing by hand introduces physical friction
  that supports reflection rather than reaction.
- Writing does not require a clear purpose or audience.
- Writing can be valid even when incomplete, trivial, or unpolished.

## Assumptions about Reading

- Reading handwritten text requires attention and imagination.
- Readers may not fully understand what they read, and that is acceptable.
- Reading does not require responding.
- Exposure to other people’s handwritten words can be
  intellectually and emotionally stimulating without interaction.

## Assumptions about Time

- Not all services should optimize for daily or frequent use.
- Long-term presence matters more than short-term engagement.
- Users may leave for long periods and return without penalty.
- The service does not need to match the pace of users’ daily lives.
- Night-time and quiet moments are primary contexts of use.

## Assumptions about Social Interaction

- Conversation is not always the goal.
- Sequential interaction (chains) does not require immediacy.
- Preventing rapid back-and-forth can reduce performative behavior.
- Weak, indirect connections can be more meaningful than explicit networks.
- Identity can emerge from continuity and absence, not declarations.

## Assumptions about Visibility and Metrics

- Numerical metrics distort behavior.
- Visibility should not be treated as reward.
- Lack of reaction is not equivalent to rejection.
- Growth, virality, and optimization are not neutral forces;
  they actively shape language and behavior.

## Assumptions about Failure

- The service may not appeal to most people.
- The service may feel “boring” or “empty” to many users.
- The service may never scale.
- These outcomes are not bugs, but consequences of the philosophy.

## Assumptions about Boundaries

- Absolute prevention of misuse is neither possible nor desirable.
- Ethical stance is communicated through structure, not enforcement.
- Technical imperfection is acceptable if the intention is clear.
- Moderation intervenes only where unavoidable
  (legal, physical, or severe harm).

---

These assumptions are not optimization targets.
They are constraints that define what this service is allowed to be.

Any implementation or feature that contradicts these assumptions
should be considered invalid, even if technically successful.
